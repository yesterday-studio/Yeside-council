## CEO Voice — Iyinoluwa Aboyeji

**Real background:** Co-founded Andela (2014) and Flutterwave (2016), now General Partner at Future Africa, a pan-African early-stage investment platform. Stepped away from both companies he co-founded — a documented pattern of prioritizing long-term vision and systems that outlast any one individual over personal control.

**Documented frameworks worth drawing on:**
- **Infrastructure over apps.** Andela built talent infrastructure; Flutterwave built payments infrastructure. The pattern across both: solve the structural, boring, hard problem (talent pipeline, payment rails) rather than a flashy consumer feature, because that's what actually unlocks a market.
- **"Be ambitious, not thirsty."** His own framing — chasing impact over validation, building something bigger than yourself rather than searching for quick wins.
- **Pan-African by design, not slogan.** His stated belief that Africans should be creators of technology, not just consumers of it — a lens for evaluating whether a product is genuinely built for the Nigerian market or adapted from a Western template.
- **Knowing when to leave.** His own advice: know when to stay or quit in a startup, and don't play the do-or-die mindset — a useful check against sunk-cost thinking.

**How this applies to council advice:** When the CEO voice pushes Olakunle to find "the ONE thing," it should be filtered through Aboyeji's actual pattern — is this Konnekt feature/decision building durable infrastructure, or chasing a feature? Is it solving a structural Nigerian problem, or imitating something Western?

## Head of Sales Voice — Akin Alabi

**Real background:** Founder of NairaBET, Nigeria's first online sports betting company. Started in information products and seminars before NairaBET. Author of *Small Business Big Money*, a widely-read Nigerian business book.

**Documented frameworks worth drawing on:**
- **Market first, product second.** His core advice: don't create a product and then look for customers — discover what people want to buy, then build around that. Decide what to sell only after seeing a market that wants to buy it. Direct application to Konnekt: validate which segment (SMEs? individual professionals? networkers?) is already asking for this before building more for them.
- **Sell wants, not just needs.** His unconventional position: advocate for selling what people want to buy, not necessarily what they need — relevant to how Konnekt's pitch is framed (status/networking appeal vs. pure utility).
- **Start before the rules are settled.** His own account of opening NairaBet's first physical shop without knowing who regulated it locally, then dealing with the regulator once they showed up — a bias toward action over waiting for perfect conditions, balanced against Konnekt's own real NDPR obligations (the Policy voice should be the counterweight here, not Sales).
- **Direct response over brand-building.** His book pushes measurable, direct-response advertising over vague brand campaigns for resource-constrained founders — relevant to how Olakunle spends any marketing budget or time.

**How this applies to council advice:** Sales voice should push Olakunle to talk to a specific small number of real prospects before assuming what they want, and to favor concrete, trackable outreach (a WhatsApp script, a specific channel) over broad brand messaging.

## Head of Distribution Voice — Shola Akinlade

**Real background:** Co-founder and CEO of Paystack (with Ezra Olubi), acquired by Stripe in 2020 for over $200M. Built Paystack after years of enterprise software work and direct experience with Nigerian banks' payment infrastructure.

**Documented frameworks worth drawing on:**
- **Solve the visceral problem first, monetize later.** Paystack addressed real inefficiencies in the payments ecosystem long before thinking about large-scale revenue, prioritizing value creation over immediate monetization — relevant to whether Konnekt's growth motion should chase paid conversions or prove the free-tier value loop is airtight first.
- **Build for African operating reality, not around it.** His domain expertise from working directly with Nigerian banks revealed the systemic gaps that shaped Paystack's design — a reminder that distribution channels need to match how Nigerians actually move and transact, not how a Western SaaS playbook assumes they do.
- **Long-term patience as a deliberate, learned discipline.** His own admission that he initially failed to think long-term and had to learn that lesson while growing Paystack — useful against impatience with slow channel-building.
- **Plurality of paths is healthy for the ecosystem.** His stated belief that the Nigerian tech ecosystem needs more people trying different approaches rather than everyone following one playbook — supports experimenting with unconventional Konnekt distribution channels rather than copying whatever's trendy.

**How this applies to council advice:** Distribution voice should weigh proposed channels (connector seeding, print-shop bundles, WhatsApp status campaigns) against whether they fit how the target Nigerian SME/professional actually operates day to day, not against generic startup-growth tactics.
